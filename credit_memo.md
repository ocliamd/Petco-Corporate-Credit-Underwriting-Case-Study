# Credit Memo: Petco Health & Wellness Company, Inc. (Nasdaq: WOOF)

**Prepared as a resume case study — illustrative credit analysis, not an actual investment recommendation.**

## Executive Summary

Petco is the second-largest pet specialty retailer in the U.S. (~1,382
stores), operating in a competitive, low-margin category against Chewy,
PetSmart, Amazon, and Walmart. Petco returned to the public markets
through an IPO in January 2021 after its 2016 leveraged buyout, while
retaining a leveraged capital structure. FY2025 results show real
operational improvement — leverage fell from 4.2x to 3.0x (Petco's own
reported net-debt basis) and free cash flow nearly quadrupled — and a
February 2026 refinancing pushed the maturity wall from 2028 to 2031. On
a **base case** extending that improvement, credit metrics continue to
strengthen through FY2030. On a **downside case** where the recent sales
decline resumes instead of reversing, gross secured leverage climbs to
6.6x and crosses this analyst's illustrative 5.5x internal warning
threshold within four years — and even senior secured lenders would not
be made whole at recovery multiples below ~6.5x EBITDA.

**Recommendation:** Financeable at the current secured level given the
demonstrated deleveraging trend and extended maturity runway, but the
credit is more sensitive to a demand-side setback than the improving
headline trend suggests — see Downside Case and Recovery Analysis below.

## Business Overview

- **Company:** Petco Health & Wellness Company, Inc. (Nasdaq: WOOF)
- **Business:** Pet specialty retail — food, supplies, grooming, and
  veterinary services across ~1,382 U.S. stores.
- **Competitive position:** #2 player behind PetSmart, facing structural
  pressure from Chewy (e-commerce) and mass merchants (Walmart, Amazon)
  on the commoditized consumables side of the business.
- **Capital structure history:** Taken private in a 2016 leveraged
  buyout (CVC Capital Partners and CPP Investments, ~$4.7bn transaction
  value), then returned to public markets via a traditional IPO in
  January 2021 while retaining meaningful funded debt from the LBO.
  ([Petco's 2021 IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1826470/000119312521009268/d62974d424b4.htm))

## Historical Financial Performance (Actual, per Petco Public Filings)

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Net sales | ~$6,154mm | $6,000mm |
| Adjusted EBITDA | ~$336.5mm | $408.2mm |
| Operating income | $7.1mm | $120.4mm |
| Net income | $(101.8)mm | $9.1mm |
| Free cash flow | ~$49.7mm | $187.0mm |
| Leverage ratio (net debt / Adj. EBITDA, company-reported) | 4.2x | 3.0x |

FY2025 marks a genuine inflection: EBITDA up 21%, free cash flow nearly
4x, and leverage down more than a full turn — all while net sales
continued to decline slightly (-2.5%), meaning the improvement came
from cost discipline and margin recovery rather than growth.

## Capital Structure (Actual, Post-February 2026 Refinancing)

| Tranche | Principal | Rate | Amortization | Maturity | Lien Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Lien Term Loan B | $900mm | SOFR + 4.25% | 1%/year | 2031 | See note below |
| Senior Secured Notes | $600mm | 8.250% fixed | Bullet | 2031 | 1st-priority fixed assets / 2nd-priority current assets |
| **Total secured debt** | **$1,500mm** | | | | |
| Operating lease liabilities (PV) | ~$1,344mm | — | — | — | — |

**Lien structure note:** per Petco's actual notes pricing disclosure,
the Senior Secured Notes carry a **split lien** — first-priority on
fixed assets, second-priority on current assets, subject to the
company's ABL-style priority structure — likely the mirror image of how
the Term Loan B is secured. This is **not** simple pari passu across all
collateral, and this memo's recovery analysis simplifies the mechanic by
allocating recovery pro rata between the two tranches rather than
modeling the fixed-asset/current-asset split explicitly (see Recovery
Analysis limitations below).
([Petco's notes pricing announcement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1826470/000119312526019795/woof-ex99_1.htm))

The refinancing extended the maturity wall by three years (2028 → 2031)
at the cost of a meaningfully higher blended coupon (~8.4% vs. the prior
facility), a fair trade for reduced near-term refinancing risk.
([Petco's February 2026 refinancing filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1826470/000119312526033607/woof-20260202.htm))

with management's stated expectation of "a return to positive comps in

## Downside Case: Can This Company Service Its Debt If Things Go Wrong?

Assumes the FY2024/FY2025 sales decline **resumes** rather than
reverses (-3%/year), with margin compression from promotional pricing
pressure rather than continued discipline, and capex held flat in
dollar terms.

| Year | Net Sales | Adj. EBITDA | Gross Secured Leverage | Simplified Cash-Flow Proxy* | Exceeds 5.5x Threshold? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2026 | $5,820mm | $367mm | 4.09x | $111mm | No |
| FY2027 | $5,645mm | $327mm | 4.55x | $73mm | No |
| FY2028 | $5,476mm | $290mm | 5.11x | $36mm | No |
| FY2029 | $5,312mm | $255mm | 5.78x | $2mm | **Yes** |
| FY2030 | $5,152mm | $222mm | 6.61x | $(31)mm | **Yes** |

*Simplified cash-flow proxy = Adj. EBITDA minus capex minus debt
service (cash interest + mandatory amortization) only. It does **not**
include cash taxes, working capital movements, restructuring costs, or
other cash uses — a real liquidity assessment would need all of these.

**Key finding:** the credit has roughly a **four-year runway** before
gross secured leverage crosses this analyst's illustrative internal
warning threshold under this downside — not an immediate risk, but a
real one if the turnaround narrative doesn't hold. This is **not** a
claim that Petco would breach its actual credit agreement covenants in
FY2029; it references only the illustrative 5.5x threshold defined for
this case study. The simplified cash-flow proxy also turns negative the
following year, meaning the company would likely need to draw on
liquidity or seek an amendment before reaching that point.

## Recovery Analysis (Assuming an Illustrative Stress Point at FY2029)

Enterprise value estimated by applying a distressed EV/EBITDA multiple
to the downside case's FY2029 Adjusted EBITDA ($255mm), then allocated
by seniority: secured debt first (pro rata between Term Loan B and
Notes, as a simplification — see limitation below), then an estimated
unsecured claims pool (capped lease rejection claims + trade payables),
then equity.

| Exit Multiple | Enterprise Value | Secured Recovery | Unsecured Recovery | Equity Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.5x | $1,148mm | 77.9% | 0% | $0 |
| 5.5x | $1,402mm | 95.2% | 0% | $0 |
| 6.5x | $1,658mm | 100% | 46.1% | $0 |
| 7.5x | $1,912mm | 100% | 100% | $40mm |

**Key finding:** even senior secured creditors are not guaranteed full
recovery in this structure — at exit multiples below ~6.5x EBITDA, both
the Term Loan B and Notes take a proportional haircut in this model. The
exit multiple assumption is the single biggest driver of the entire
recovery outcome, which is exactly why it needs to be stress-tested
explicitly rather than assumed as a single point estimate.

**Important limitations on this recovery analysis:**
- The pro-rata split between Term Loan B and Notes is a **simplification**.
  Per Petco's actual disclosures, the Notes carry a split lien
  (first-priority fixed assets / second-priority current assets),
  likely mirrored by the Term Loan B — actual recovery could differ
  meaningfully between the two tranches depending on how enterprise
  value breaks down between fixed-asset and current-asset value, which
  isn't modeled here.
- This waterfall **excludes** DIP financing (which typically primes
  existing claims in an actual Chapter 11), administrative claims,
  professional fees, transaction costs, and tax consequences of a
  restructuring. Real-world recoveries would likely be lower across the
  board once these priority claims and costs are included.

## Limitations & Methodology Notes

- Base case and downside case growth/margin assumptions are the
  analyst's own, built off Petco's actual FY2025 results and qualitative
  management commentary — not Petco guidance for specific out-year figures.
- The 5.5x level referenced throughout is an **illustrative internal
  leverage warning threshold** set by this analyst for this case study —
  it is **not** Petco's actual disclosed maintenance covenant level, and
  should not be read as a real covenant default under Petco's actual
  credit agreement.
- The $400mm unsecured claims pool is a simplification; actual lease
  rejection damages in a real bankruptcy are capped by statute and would
  require deal-specific analysis to size precisely.
- Leverage is presented on a **gross secured-debt basis** throughout
  (not net of cash) — a deliberately conservative choice that differs
  numerically from Petco's own reported ratio, which nets against cash
  on hand.
- SOFR is held static at 4.3% for the Term Loan B's floating rate;
  actual interest expense will vary with the real rate path.
- The recovery waterfall's secured pro-rata assumption simplifies away
  the actual split-lien structure between the Term Loan B and Notes;
  see Recovery Analysis limitations above.

## Recommendation

The credit is financeable at the current secured level: real
deleveraging progress, an extended maturity runway to 2031, and adequate
coverage under the base case. The key risk to monitor is whether the
FY2025 margin-driven improvement is durable if top-line pressure
resumes — the downside case shows this credit has real, but not
unlimited, room to absorb a renewed sales decline before leverage and
liquidity stress emerge against this analyst's own illustrative
thresholds.
